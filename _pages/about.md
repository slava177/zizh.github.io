I am a fifth-year PhD student at Carnegie Mellon University studying Electrical and Computer Engineering, advised by Prof. Guannan Qu and Prof. Yorie Nakahira. I obtained my M.S. degree at Duke University in Computer Science in 2022. I obtained my B.S. degree in Honors math and computer science at University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, in 2021. 

My current research interests are in stochastic control, reinforcement learning, and diffusion models. I am broadly interested in graph theory, algebraic topology and their applications in machine learning.

On diffusion models, I work on both theory and applications. We showed that Riemannian diffusion models, which generate data lying on a manifold, need only a polynomially small step size to sample accurately ([ICLR 2026]({{ base_path }}/publication/2026-riemannian-diffusion)). I also developed HilbertGen-3D, a topology-aware diffusion model for 3D shape generation that is conditioned on multifractal statistics gathered along a 3D Hilbert curve ([NeurIPS 2026]({{ base_path }}/publication/2026-hilbertgen-3d)).
